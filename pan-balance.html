    Practice desk

    This is the same desk as the Mix Trainer. Turn the pan knobs and move the faders, and watch the radar and the balance meter. Keep instruments inside the red marks: hard left and right are for the reverb returns. Drag a knob, use the arrow keys, or double-click to centre it. Each fader is treated as how loud that part sounds, and pans use the common −3 dB pan law. The checks compare your settings with the books' rules. It's a rough guide, not a meter on real audio.

        Building the balance

        Balance means every important sound is clearly audible at the right level, all the way through (Senior). Panning comes first because moving a pan changes the level by 3–6 dB (the pan law) and undoes your fader work (Savage). Then bring the faders in one group at a time.

        Numbers & disagreements

        WhatValueBook

        Mono: the books disagree. Savage says mono compatibility can be ignored when choosing pans (still check mono for EQ and phase). Senior, Oltheten and Owsinski check panning in mono, and Oltheten calls mono "the most robust mix; stereo is the icing".

        How wide: the books disagree. Savage and Clarke use the whole image and hard pans freely. Oltheten keeps the important parts about equal in both speakers and lets reverbs and secondary parts make the width. Owsinski warns against stacking every stereo source hard left and right (Big Mono).

        Whose view. Drummer's view or audience's view both work; Savage says pick one and keep the hi-hat, overheads and toms consistent with it. Savage's table below uses hi-hat right, floor tom left.

        Live room (not from the books). These books are about studio mixes. In a church, many seats are much closer to one speaker, and some systems run in mono. Hard-panned parts may be missing for half the room, so lean on the mono check and on Oltheten's advice to keep essentials near the middle.

        Our team rule. The band builder and practice desk keep every instrument inside about 80%, and leave hard left and right for the reverb returns. That follows Oltheten's approach (important parts near equal in both speakers, reverbs and secondary parts make the width) rather than Savage's and Clarke's hard pans, which the plans above still show as the books give them.
