    Sends: inserts and side roads

    Groups

    A group is one fader for a family of instruments. Build the balance first, then send each family to its group. You then process and rebalance fewer things.

    How many. Benediktsson: don't end up with more groups than source tracks. Oltheten keeps to about 16 faders or fewer so the mix fits in your head.

    Stacked compression. Savage: drums may pass through three compressors (track, drum group, mix bus). Together they can turn excitement into listener fatigue.

    Reverb returns. Nyquist sends a return to the master and not through a group compressor, because compression kills the sense of space. The exception is the drum reverb, which goes through the drum group.

    Numbers

    WhatValueBook

    Starting level: the books differ. Benediktsson starts tracks around −11 dB. Oltheten lets percussive sources peak at −6 dBFS and others as low as −12 dBFS. Savage starts with drums peaking near −10 dB on the master. All three leave room to rebalance.

    At church (not from the books). These books are about studio mixing. On a live desk, set gain with the real singer or player at performance level, because a soundcheck is often quieter than the service. Monitor mixes normally use pre-fader sends, so your front-of-house fader moves don't change what the band hears. Check how your own desk is set, since this varies.

    A clip stays clipped (not from the books). Once the input stage overloads, the damage is in the signal, so a later fader only makes the distortion quieter. Inside a computer mix, Senior notes channels have 24 dB or more of headroom. That headroom doesn't help a mic that already overloaded on the way in.
