Mastering Map

Mastering makes a finished mix ready for its listeners. It evens out level and tone across songs and prepares the files. It can't create what isn't in the mix. Savage's default chain is a transparent EQ and a brickwall limiter, and everything else is a specialty tool.

Mastering EQ moves

A mastering EQ touches every instrument at once, so the moves are tiny. Savage rarely goes past ±2 dB in a whole session and hears 0.5 dB clearly. This graph only spans ±3 dB. The same scale would make a channel-EQ boost run off the chart.

Savage's workflow

Master one reference song, then match the rest to it. EQ comes before level because EQ changes level.

      WhatValueBook

      Ceiling: the books disagree. Savage normally sets about −0.2 dBFS. Oltheten keeps peaks at −1 dBTP or lower for normalised streaming.

      How loud: the books disagree. Savage aims for "moderate", 3–6 dB of limiting. Oltheten uses no more limiting than the platform target needs. Valeriote takes approval mixes of heavy music to about −12 dB RMS. Senior gives no target and matches references by ear.

      Limiter on the mix: the books disagree. Oltheten delivers with no limiter and uses one only as a test. Savage says mixing through a limiter protects the kick and snare. Valeriote uses a temporary limiter for approval, then bypasses it before mastering.

      Chain order. Savage: filter, EQ, optional compression, limiter. Nyquist: EQ, bus compressor, limiter. Senior puts bus EQ after the bus compressor. Valeriote's buss chain has no EQ by default: compressor, then loudness.

      Dates. Savage's book is from 2014, and platform targets and upload formats change. Check each platform's current spec before you deliver. Owsinski's 1999 edition has no mastering chapter; Owsinski's part here is printing alternate mixes.

      At church (not from the books). These books are about recordings. Nothing here applies to the live room mix on a Sunday. Use this page for an EP, a song release, or a service recording you plan to publish.
